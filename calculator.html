🧮 Hit Dice Calculator

Tool 1 of 20 · Part of the free 5e HP Forge toolkit · Runs offline, no tracking

The Hit Dice Calculator is the heart of the toolkit. Type a hit-dice expression such as 8d10+24 or 15d6-5, press Calculate HP, and you get the complete picture of that creature's hit points instantly. The parser reads the standard NdM±K notation used on every official D&D 5e statblock, so you can copy an HP line straight out of a published adventure, paste it in, and see the numbers behind it.

The result grid answers the questions that matter at the table: the fixed average a DM can use without rolling, the minimum if every die lands on a 1, and the maximum if the creature rolls beautifully. Below the grid, the breakdown shows the arithmetic step by step — how many dice, what each die contributes on average, and where the flat bonus comes from — so you can verify the math instead of trusting a black box.

The Batch Multi-Entry card is the prep-night time-saver. Paste a whole list of expressions, separated by newlines, semicolons or commas, and every one is calculated in a single pass. It is the fastest way to stat a full encounter group, sanity-check a chapter's worth of homebrew creatures, or build a custom bestiary table before Sunday's session.

What you can do

  • One-click average, minimum and maximum HP for any NdM±K expression
  • A transparent, dice-by-dice breakdown you can verify or explain to players
  • Batch mode for newline, semicolon or comma separated lists in one pass
  • Works with monster hit dice and player-class dice alike, d4 through d20
  • Instant client-side results — no reload, no network, works offline
  • Hand the same expression to the Probability Dashboard or Comparison Mode

How the math works

For N dice of size M plus modifier K, the average is N × (M+1) ÷ 2 + K. A classic 8d10+24 brute therefore averages 8 × 5.5 + 24 = 68 HP — exactly the number printed in published statblocks, because that is the same convention. The minimum is N + K and the maximum is N × M + K, giving the honest floor and ceiling of the pool: 32 to 104 for that same expression. Use the average for encounter math, and the floor when you want to know how bad a bad roll really is.

Prep tip: published statblocks use the fixed average, so your result should match the book exactly. If it doesn't, check the die count before blaming the tool — a swapped die size is the most common typo.
