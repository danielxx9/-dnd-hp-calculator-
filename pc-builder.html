🧝 PC Hit Point Builder

Tool 5 of 20 · Part of the free 5e HP Forge toolkit · Runs offline, no tracking

Player characters deserve the same rigorous math as monsters. The PC Builder computes a character's hit points at any level under either ruleset — the 2024 Player's Handbook or the legacy 2014 rules — across all thirteen classes: Barbarian on a d12; Fighter, Paladin and Ranger on d10s; Monk, Rogue, Bard, Cleric, Druid, Warlock and Artificer on d8s; Wizard and Sorcerer on d6s.

Building a multiclass character? Add one row per class and set its levels — the tool applies each class's dice correctly and adds the Constitution bonus every level. Feats and racial features slot in as checkboxes: Tough grants +2 HP per level, Hill Dwarf +1, and the Draconic Bloodline sorcerer +1. A custom per-level field covers everything else, from epic boons to DM gifts.

The level-by-level breakdown expands below the result, showing exactly how the character grows from level 1 to 20. It is invaluable for sanity-checking a new sheet, comparing a durable Fighter against a same-level Barbarian, or settling the eternal 'does Tough beat an ASI?' debate with numbers instead of forum posts.

What you can do

  • 2014 and 2024 rulesets, each with its correct progression
  • All thirteen official classes with their hit dice
  • Multiclass rows with per-class levels
  • Tough, Hill Dwarf and Draconic toggles plus a custom bonus
  • Level-by-level breakdown from 1st to 20th
  • Constitution modifier applied at every level including the first

How the math works

Under the 2014 rules a character takes the maximum on the first class die, then the fixed average — 4.5 on a d8, 5.5 on a d10 — or a roll, plus Constitution, every level. The 2024 progression replaces rolls with published fixed values per level, so two players building the same character always land on the same number.

Because every term is explicit, a player can verify their own character sheet in seconds, and the DM can catch a misapplied level-up or a forgotten Con bump before it hardens into a campaign-long error.

Multiclass note: your first class level always takes the maximum die rather than the average — the tool handles that ordering automatically, whichever class you list first.

Open this tool

PC Hit Point Builder lives inside the main calculator. Open the app and click the PC Builder tab — the fifth one in the tab bar. Everything computes client-side and works offline once the page has loaded.
