🎲 Monte Carlo Combat Simulator

Tool 19 of 20 · Part of the free 5e HP Forge toolkit · Runs offline, no tracking

Before you put a boss in front of your players, let a thousand alternate universes fight it first. The Monte Carlo Combat Simulator compresses each side into combat-relevant stats — the party's total HP, DPR and average AC against the monsters' total HP, DPR and attack bonus — then plays out complete fights, rolling every attack and every hit, hundreds or thousands of times.

Set the simulation count anywhere from 100 to 10,000, and include party size and monster count so the action economy is priced in — the difference between four heroes and one is structural, not just a stat line. The results are statistics you can act on: the party's win rate, the casualty rate among survivors, and the distribution of how many rounds fights actually lasted, drawn as a chart so the shape is as visible as the numbers.

This is the tool for the questions the Encounter Builder cannot answer. A 'deadly' boss against a high-damage, low-HP party might win 90% of simulations — or 40%. Run it, nudge the monster's HP or AC one notch, run it again, and you have tuned the fight to the drama you want: hard, winnable, never a coin-flip TPK.

What you can do

  • 100 to 10,000 complete combat simulations per run
  • Win rate and casualty rate reported separately
  • Round-count distribution chart of fight length
  • Party size and monster count for true action economy
  • Attack rolls, AC and HP all modeled, not just DPR math
  • Tuning loop: change one stat, re-run, compare

How the math works

Each simulation rolls d20 attack checks against AC and applies damage until one side reaches zero. A single fight is noise; aggregated over hundreds of runs, variance averages out and the matchup's underlying balance shows through — which is why the win rate converges as you raise the simulation count.

Read the casualties: a 90% win rate where the healer dies in most runs is a different encounter than a 90% walkover. The casualty rate is where the drama hides.

Open this tool

Monte Carlo Combat Simulator lives inside the main calculator. Open the app and click the Monte Carlo tab — the nineteenth one in the tab bar. Everything computes client-side and works offline once the page has loaded.
