📊 Probability Dashboard

Tool 6 of 20 · Part of the free 5e HP Forge toolkit · Runs offline, no tracking

Averages hide variance. Two expressions can land in the same neighborhood while one is a smooth bell curve and the other a spiky lottery. The Probability Dashboard draws the real distribution of any hit-dice expression as a histogram, so you can see which outcomes are common, which are outliers, and where the mass of the curve actually sits.

For a handful of dice the distribution is computed exactly, every outcome tallied — true probabilities, not a statistical shortcut. For pools too large to enumerate, it falls back to a 5,000-roll simulation, clearly labeled as approximate. Below the chart, Roll Once samples a single instant and Simulate 1000 Rolls runs a thousand virtual rolls so you can watch the empirical results converge on the exact curve.

Use it to make design decisions with confidence: is this boss's HP pool reliably scary, or sometimes trivially low? Does a reroll actually change the shape of the curve, or just shift the mean? It also makes a fine teaching aid when a player wants to know why their greatsword crit feels so swingy.

What you can do

  • Exact probability histogram for any expression the calculator accepts
  • Automatic fallback to a labeled 5,000-roll simulation for huge pools
  • Single-roll sampler for instant table feel
  • 1,000-roll simulation with empirical-versus-exact comparison
  • Canvas-drawn chart that renders fully offline
  • Works for monster HP pools and damage expressions alike

How the math works

The average of N dice of size M concentrates around N × (M+1) ÷ 2, but the spread shrinks as dice are added: one d20 is nearly flat, while twenty of them pile into a narrow spike around the mean. Same average, completely different reliability — and the histogram is how you see that before it surprises the table.

The mean is printed above the chart, and bar heights are proportional to true probability, so genuinely rare outcomes are visibly dwarfed by the common middle of the curve — the shape does the explaining for you.

Reading the chart: compare the width, not just the peak. A tall, narrow curve means dependable numbers; a low, wide one means the encounter swings on luck.

Open this tool
